UX research · HCI · IAT 351

Whisper — Classroom Participation.

A mobile participation concept designed to help students ask questions more comfortably while giving instructors a structured way to review, prioritize, and respond without disrupting a lecture.

01

Problem space

Class participation can create both social and cognitive pressure.

Students may have a useful question but remain silent because of anxiety, fear of judgment, or unequal classroom power dynamics.

During live lectures, students are already listening, interpreting, taking notes, and preparing responses. Adding a public interaction can increase cognitive load. Instructors face a parallel challenge: reviewing a large stream of questions without breaking the rhythm of teaching.

Design opportunity

Create a low-friction, anonymous participation system that helps students contribute safely and helps instructors organize questions without being overwhelmed.

03

Research foundation

Three waves of HCI shaped the design rationale.

Wave 1

Perception, cognition, and motor effort

Visual hierarchy, Gestalt grouping, cognitive-load management, affordances, feedback, and large tap targets reduce effort during fast-paced lectures.

Wave 2

Distributed activity and mental models

Shared question states, tagging, clustering, incoming/outgoing tabs, and familiar messaging patterns support coordination between students and instructors.

Wave 3

Experience and psychological safety

Anonymous participation, a soft visual tone, predictable feedback, and non-intrusive interactions lower social risk and support a sense of belonging.

01

Anonymity needs safety and moderation

Anonymous tools can encourage expression, but only when the space feels controlled, respectful, and well moderated.

02

Relevance protects attention

Excess or poorly structured input increases cognitive load. Filtering, clustering, and limited visible information help users focus on what matters.

03

Predictable patterns reduce onboarding

Role selection, class cards, question feeds, status labels, and familiar messaging actions align with existing mental models.

04

Information architecture

One shared system, with distinct teacher and student paths.

05

Prototype walkthrough

The screens turn the research into a simple classroom workflow.

01 · Onboarding

Clear setup before entering a class

The first screens establish identity, account information, and role. Large fields and cards reduce fine-motor demand, while the teacher and student choice reflects a familiar classroom mental model.

02 · Class setup

A guided path from an empty state to an active class

The empty-state message makes the next action explicit. Related setup fields are grouped using proximity, and the primary action is visually emphasized to make progression predictable.

03 · Instructor workflow

Create, organize, and review classroom questions

Incoming and outgoing tabs create a clear information boundary. Question cards use status cues, categories, and familiar actions so an instructor can scan the feed without losing teaching flow.

04 · Student participation

Find relevant questions and respond with low friction

Unread, starred, and similar filters provide information scent and reduce search effort. The active question screen limits unnecessary options so the student can focus on one response at a time.

06

Reflection and next steps

The strongest design decisions came from linking theory to context.

Whisper helped me understand how UX research can move beyond a list of findings and become a design rationale: every filter, status cue, role choice, and interaction pattern should respond to a real cognitive, social, or emotional need.

Validate the workflow

Test task completion, mental effort, and interaction accuracy with students and instructors.

Refine moderation

Explore how clustering, upvoting, and instructor controls can prevent duplicate or distracting questions.

Improve accessibility

Review contrast, keyboard support, screen-reader labels, and motion feedback in a higher-fidelity prototype.
