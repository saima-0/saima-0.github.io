01

Overview

Turning a large movie dataset into a visual story.

The team created an interactive website that helps people compare three major studios without needing to interpret a spreadsheet or move between disconnected charts.

The problem

Movie performance is often discussed through assumptions: a popular studio must be the most profitable, a highly rated movie must earn more, or one genre always performs best. Raw datasets made those questions difficult to investigate quickly.

The objective

We aimed to help movie enthusiasts and data-focused users explore which genres perform best, how the studios compare, and how audience ratings relate to box-office revenue.

02

Research and data

One dataset was not enough to answer the full question.

The main dataset contained broad movie metadata, but it did not provide every studio-specific field we needed. We combined multiple Kaggle and TMDB-related sources to support comparisons of revenue, ratings, popularity, studios, and genres from 2000–2024.

Research challenge

Missing values and inconsistent studio information affected how complete some visualizations could be. The team had to compare sources and make careful decisions about what could be shown.

04

Design iterations

Small visual decisions had a large effect on comprehension.

01

A clearer studio colour system

DC and Pixar initially used similar blue tones. We changed DC to green and aligned the tri-variate visualization with the same studio colour system, reducing ambiguity across the website.

02

Larger type and charts

The body type was increased from 16px to 20px, and the cumulative-profit graph was enlarged. This made labels and differences between studios easier to compare.

03

More intentional interaction cues

We identified hover effects that suggested clickability where no action existed. A future revision would remove misleading cues, add legend-based filtering, and strengthen the annotation for the 2023–2024 revenue decline.

05

Outcome and reflection

The final experience became easier to read and easier to trust.

A stronger colour system, larger type, clearer annotations, and more space around the visualizations helped the website communicate its findings without overwhelming the user.

Clearer comparison

Studio colours remained more predictable across the visual story.

Improved readability

Larger labels and charts reduced unnecessary visual effort.

Better context

The Covid-19 annotation explained a major revenue dip rather than leaving users to guess.

“Data visualization is not only about making charts. It is about helping people understand the story behind the data.”
